# Ending and the Recording

## The reveal

After the final case, Elara's understanding of the project is overturned. The player learns that the cases were not ordinary diseases and that the compounds developed throughout the campaign were used by the military to disable the Vaelith immune system.

Lucien uses a recording of a conversation he had with a Vaelith woman to taunt Elara. The recording is not merely a detached testimony: it captures Lucien speaking directly to the woman, mocking her, and revealing Elara's identity as the researcher responsible for developing the compounds.

## The recording's emotional progression

The intended progression is:

1. **Mockery and desperation.** Lucien mocks the woman. She begins by pleading for mercy.
2. **The truth is made personal.** Lucien reveals Elara's name and explains that she developed the compounds used against the Vaelith.
3. **Grief becomes anger.** The woman condemns Lucien and Elara, blaming them for the deaths of her children and her husband.
4. **A final curse.** She curses both of them and warns that they will not get away with what they have done.

The woman's anger should feel earned. Her words are not a villain monologue; they are the response of someone who has lost her family and has been forced to confront the people responsible.

## Dialogue direction

The following is a tone and beat outline, not finalized dialogue:

- Lucien opens with calm, deliberate mockery.
- The woman asks or begs him to stop and show mercy.
- Lucien tells her that the researcher responsible for the compounds is Dr. Elara Voss.
- The woman reacts to learning the name of the person whose work caused such devastation.
- She condemns both Lucien, who ordered or enabled the operation, and Elara, whose compounds were used to carry it out.
- She speaks of her dead children and husband.
- She ends with a warning along the lines of: **"You won't get away with this."**

The recording should preserve the distinction between responsibility and intent: Elara did develop the compounds, but she did not know what they were being used for. Lucien knows the truth and deliberately weaponizes that information to hurt her.

## Presentation notes

- Keep the woman unnamed, including in subtitles and internal character labels shown to the player.
- The recording should feel like an actual conversation, with interruptions and reactions, not two disconnected speeches.
- Lucien should remain composed. His cruelty is most effective when he does not need to shout.
- Give the woman's grief room to turn into anger; avoid making the entire recording one continuous scream.
- Do not resolve the player's emotional response for them with an overly explanatory speech.

## Open narrative decisions

- Does Elara hear the recording immediately after the final case, or does Lucien first frame it as a routine debrief?
- Does Elara respond verbally, through an interaction, or remain silent?
- Does the game end immediately after the recording, or allow a brief final playable moment?
- What exactly does Lucien believe justifies the operation?
